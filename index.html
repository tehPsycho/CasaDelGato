About Collecting Antiques

Antique collecting is part history, part craft, and part storytelling. Focus on learning a piece's age, maker, condition, materials, and regional style before you buy.

  • Start with periods: Georgian, Victorian, Arts & Crafts, Art Nouveau, Art Deco, and Mid-Century Modern.
  • Check condition honestly: look for repairs, replacements, heavy wear, and missing pieces.
  • Provenance matters: old receipts, family records, and labels add context and value.

Identification & Classification Guides

Art Period Basics

Learn quick visual clues for major decorative eras and pottery styles.

  • Art Nouveau: flowing botanical lines.
  • Art Deco: symmetry, geometry, bold contrast.
  • Mid-Century: clean forms and minimal ornament.

Silver & Tin Workmen Stamps

Use marks, plate wear, and pattern naming to separate plated from sterling pieces.

  • Look for maker name, lion/passant symbols, or numeric standards.
  • EPNS / A1 often indicates silverplate.
  • Compare stamp style with documented hallmark charts.

Wood Furniture Marks

Inspect underside and back panels for stamps, chalk notations, and joinery clues.

  • Hand-cut dovetails suggest older craftsmanship.
  • Machine-perfect joints usually indicate newer production.
  • Patina should match wear patterns naturally.

Pottery Maker Spotting

Great for McKoy, Bauer, Luray, and other art pottery families.

  • Record base marks exactly as seen.
  • Check glaze color and shape catalogs.
  • Watch for recasts and modern reproductions.
