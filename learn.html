Value Foundations

Not every old piece is collectible, and not every collectible is antique. Learn the core vocabulary first.

Old

Age alone, with little market context or demand history.

Vintage

Usually 20+ years old and representative of a specific period.

Antique

Commonly 100+ years old in many collecting circles.

Collectible / Rare / Desirable

Driven by scarcity, demand, design quality, and buyer interest.

Major Value Factors

Age Window

Early production runs can matter more than absolute age.

Maker & Markings

Known makers and authentic marks improve confidence.

Material & Build

Sterling vs plate, solid wood vs veneer, hand-cut vs molded.

Condition

Chips, cracks, repairs, and replaced parts impact demand.

How to Evaluate an Item in 5 Steps

  1. Identify the object type and era. Look at form, motifs, hardware, and manufacturing clues.
  2. Inspect construction details. Check joints, seams, glazing, fasteners, and wear patterns.
  3. Document condition honestly. Note chips, hairlines, corrosion, warping, and restoration.
  4. Find maker marks or signatures. Photograph base, underside, backs, and closeups.
  5. Compare against known examples. Match shape, dimensions, and decoration before buying.

Condition Notes That Change Value Fast

  • Original finishes are often preferred over aggressive refinishing.
  • Stable patina can be desirable; active damage is not.
  • Professional restoration with disclosure usually outperforms hidden repair.

Research & Pricing Workflow

1) Build Keywords

Start with object + maker + material + era + motif.

2) Use Sold Comparables

Ignore asking prices; focus on sold listings and auction results.

3) Match Condition

Adjust comparisons for chips, repairs, missing parts, and finish wear.

4) Check Seasonality

Demand can shift by season, trends, and decor cycles.

Quick Pricing Bands

Use these as planning ranges before deeper comps.

  • Entry-tier: common forms, high supply, modest demand.
  • Mid-tier: known makers or cleaner examples with active demand.
  • Premium-tier: scarce forms, documented provenance, superior condition.

Common Mistakes New Collectors Make

  • Buying from one photo only.
  • Ignoring repairs, chips, and replaced parts.
  • Confusing reproductions with period originals.
  • Skipping maker-mark research and pattern matching.

Before You Buy Checklist

  • Confirm material and construction clues.
  • Request base/back/underside photos.
  • Compare sold listings and documented examples.
  • Assess condition issues against asking price.
  • Ask for dimensions and recent restoration details in writing.
