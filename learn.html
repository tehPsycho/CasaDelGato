Antique History & Learning Hub

Old does not always mean valuable. Collectors commonly evaluate maker, rarity, condition, originality, and demand before purchasing.

Old vs Valuable

Old

Age alone, little market context.

Vintage

Representative of a period, usually 20+ years.

Antique

Often 100+ years in many collecting circles.

Collectible / Rare / Desirable

Driven by scarcity, demand, and design quality.

Major Value Factors

Age

Early production windows can matter.

Maker

Known makers are easier to research.

Material

Sterling vs plate, hardwood vs veneer, etc.

Condition

Damage and repairs affect demand.

Rarity

Low survival rates can increase interest.

Provenance

Paper trail and provenance add context.

Style/period

Some periods are more researched than others.

Current demand

Market interest can vary by trend.

Completeness

Matching sets are commonly sought after.

Restoration history

Quality and disclosure matter.

Common Mistakes New Collectors Make

  • Buying from one photo only.
  • Ignoring repairs, chips, and replaced parts.
  • Confusing reproductions with period originals.
  • Skipping maker-mark research and pattern matching.

Before You Buy Checklist

  • Confirm material and construction clues.
  • Request base/back/underside photos.
  • Compare sold listings and documented examples.
  • Assess condition issues against asking price.
